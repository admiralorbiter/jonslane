Chapter 1

The Multi-Hump Track

In physical systems, potential energy landscapes are rarely simple, symmetrical valleys. They often consist of multiple hills, valleys, and barriers.

Observe this new fourth-degree polynomial landscape, featuring a deep left valley, a shallow right valley, and a central hill barrier.

Release the particle from different heights and observe how it rolls across the landscape. The physics is still frictionless, and total energy is conserved.

Chapter 2

Energy Limits

Let's cap the total energy of our system. An Amber Total Energy Line ($E$) is now displayed on the canvas.

Click and drag this energy line vertically (or grab the particle to adjust its initial release height).

Observe that the particle's maximum vertical reach matches the height of this energy line exactly. The particle rolls up the hill, decelerates, stops at the height of the energy line, and rolls back down.

If you drag the energy line below the top of the central hill, you will notice something interesting: **the particle is trapped** in one valley, unable to cross into the other.

Chapter 3

Allowed vs. Forbidden Regions

Because total energy is $E = K + U$, and kinetic energy cannot be negative ($K \ge 0$), the particle can only exist where the potential energy is less than or equal to the total energy ($U \le E$).

We can now visualize these zones on the track:

  • Allowed Regions ($E \ge U$): Highlighted in solid **Neon Cyan**. Here, the particle is permitted to travel. The translucent cyan shading above the track represents the kinetic energy ($K = E - U$) at each point.
  • Forbidden Regions ($E < U$): Highlighted as a **Dashed Grey** line and covered in a diagonal hatch pattern. Classically, the particle is physically barred from entering this zone because it would require negative kinetic energy ($K < 0$, which implies an imaginary velocity).

Drag the energy line up and down, and observe how these allowed and forbidden zones dynamically expand, shrink, split, or merge.

Chapter 4

Classical Turning Points

The boundaries between the allowed and forbidden regions are called the **classical turning points**.

At these exact intersections, $E = U$, meaning the kinetic energy is exactly zero:

$$K = 0 \implies v_s = 0$$

Turning points are represented by **Amber Brackets** `[` and `]`. Observe what happens when the particle reaches a bracket:

  1. Its speed shrinks to zero, and the Cyan velocity vector disappears.
  2. The **Amber force vector** does *not* disappear. It reaches its local maximum because the track is sloped at the bracket.

This is a crucial point: even though the particle stops momentarily, the non-zero force immediately accelerates it back into the allowed region.

Chapter 5

Crossing the Barrier

To cross the central hill, the particle needs enough energy to climb over it. Classically, the central hill acts as a potential barrier.

Drag the Total Energy Line ($E$) above the peak of the central hill. Notice that the allowed regions in the left and right valleys merge into a single, continuous allowed zone. The particle is now free to explore the entire track!

A Glimpse into Quantum Tunneling:

In classical mechanics, the forbidden zone is an absolute barrier. But in quantum mechanics, small particles behave like waves.

Click **"Enable Wave"** in the left control panel. You will see a fading purple wavefunction leaking into the forbidden barrier. If the barrier is thin enough, the wave can leak all the way through, allowing the particle to appear on the other side—without ever having enough energy to climb the hill!

Critically, the particle's total energy remains exactly the same on both sides of the barrier.

Chapter 6

Concept Check

Let's check your understanding of potential landscapes and turning points.

Question 1 (Turning Point Force):

A rolling particle reaches its turning point on a sloped track and comes to a momentary stop ($v = 0$). What is the net force on the particle at this instant?

Question 2 (Reference Shift Invariance):

If you shift the arbitrary zero of potential energy upward by +2 Joules ($U \to U + 2$), what happens to the physical locations of the turning points?

Question 3 (Tunneling Energy):

In quantum tunneling, a wave-particle crosses a forbidden barrier where its total energy is less than the potential energy ($E < U$). What is the energy of the particle after it emerges on the other side?
