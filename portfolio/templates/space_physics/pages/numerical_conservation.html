Chapter 1

The Exploding Spring

When we simulate physics on a computer, the computer does not solve differential equations continuously. Instead, it approximates motion by jumping forward in discrete steps of time ($\Delta t$).

Observe this simple mass attached to a spring ($F = -kx$). It is currently being simulated using the standard **Forward Euler** method.

Click **Play**. Watch the particle's movement. It swings wider and wider, and the red line on the energy graph curves upward exponentially.

Without any external forces or energy inputs, the computer is creating energy from nothing, violating the conservation of energy!

Chapter 2

Step Size & Drift

A **Step Size Slider ($\Delta t$)** has been unlocked on the left.

Experiment with this slider. If you make the step size larger, the spring explodes almost immediately, forcing the guardrails to halt the simulation.

If you make the step size extremely small, the spring still explodes, but it takes longer. This reveals a key insight: the energy leak is a fundamental property of the algorithm, not just a minor rounding error.

Chapter 3

The Geometry of the Overshoot

Why does Forward Euler fail? The mathematics explains it exactly.

At each step, Forward Euler uses the velocity and force at the *beginning* of the step to project the state forward:

$$x_{n+1} = x_n + v_n \Delta t$$ $$v_{n+1} = v_n - \omega^2 x_n \Delta t$$

For a spring oscillator, calculating the total energy $E_{n+1}$ at the next step reveals:

$$E_{n+1} = E_n (1 + \omega^2 \Delta t^2)$$

Because $(1 + \omega^2 \Delta t^2) > 1$ for any step size, energy **must grow** at every step.

Geometrically, the solver projects the state along a tangent vector in phase space, stepping outward from the constant energy ellipse.

Chapter 4

Euler-Cromer & Symplectic Fix

We can fix this by updating the velocity first, and then using the *new* velocity to update the position:

$$v_{n+1} = v_n - \omega^2 x_n \Delta t$$ $$x_{n+1} = x_n + v_{n+1} \Delta t$$

Select **Euler-Cromer** in the solver menu on the left.

Look at the energy graph. It is no longer exploding! The blue line wiggles periodically, but does not drift upward over time.

Euler-Cromer is a **symplectic (area-preserving)** integrator. It conserves a modified "shadow energy" exactly:

$$E_{\text{shadow}} = E_{\text{mech}} - \frac{1}{2} k \Delta t x v$$

The wiggles in mechanical energy correspond to the final cross-term oscillating as the particle slides back and forth.

Chapter 5

Verlet vs. RK4 (The Long-Term Trap)

Let's compare two advanced solvers: **Velocity Verlet** (glowing green) and **Runge-Kutta 4 (RK4)** (purple).

Velocity Verlet is a second-order symplectic solver. Like Euler-Cromer, it preserves phase-space area, but its energy wiggles are much smaller (scaling as $\mathcal{O}(\Delta t^2)$).

RK4 is a fourth-order explicit solver. It is incredibly accurate in the short term, but because it is non-symplectic, it slowly bleeds energy over time.

Click **"Phase Space"** below the canvas to compare them. Velocity Verlet draws a perfectly closed ellipse, while RK4 slowly spirals inward (artificial damping).

The Lesson: High short-term accuracy does not guarantee long-term stability. For planetary orbits or molecular bonds, symplectic integrators are essential.
