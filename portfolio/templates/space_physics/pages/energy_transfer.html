Chapter 1

The Frictional Valley

Until now, our physical models have assumed a frictionless universe. Let's introduce sliding dry friction ($\mu$) using the slider on the left.

Release the particle from the left slope. Notice how the velocity vector (Cyan) and force vector (Amber) behave.

Unlike the frictionless coaster, the particle climbs slightly lower on each return swing. Its kinetic and potential energy heights decay until it comes to a complete, static stop at the bottom.

Chapter 2

Where Did the Energy Go?

When the particle rolls with friction and eventually stops at rest, the kinetic energy ($K$) and potential energy ($U$) are both zero.

Prediction Prompt:

If energy is conserved and cannot be destroyed, where has the mechanical energy of the particle gone after it stops?

Chapter 3

Shifting to Thermal Energy

Friction does not destroy energy—it transforms it. Look at the energy chart on the left.

A third bar has been revealed: **Thermal Energy ($E_{\text{th}}$)** (Orange).

As the particle slides, observe how the mechanical energy bars ($K$ and $U$) decay, while the thermal energy bar ($E_{\text{th}}$) grows.

Look at the horizontal **solid Amber line** representing the total energy $E_{\text{total}} = K + U + E_{\text{th}}$. Even as the particle slows down and heat builds up, this line remains **perfectly flat and constant**.

The First Law of Thermodynamics holds: the total energy of our closed universe remains constant.

Chapter 4

System Boundaries & Work

To understand this conversion formally, we must define the **System Boundary**:

  • Mechanical System: Particle + Earth's Field ($E_{\text{mech}} = K + U$). It is enclosed by the dashed blue box on the chart.
  • Surroundings: The track and surrounding environment.

Because the track is in the surroundings, friction acts as an **external force** doing negative work ($W_{\text{friction}}$) on the mechanical system:

$$W_{\text{friction}} = \Delta E_{\text{mech}} < 0$$

This negative work transfers energy across the boundary, converting it to heat.

Observe the animations: orange heat sparks flow from the track contact point across the dashed boundary on the canvas, while glowing energy packets cross the dashed system border on the chart canvas.

Chapter 5

Allowed Zone Shrinkage

As mechanical energy is transferred out of our system, the **Mechanical Energy Line ($E_{\text{mech}}$)** (dashed Cyan/Magenta line) slides downward.

Look at the canvas. Because $E_{\text{mech}}$ is decaying, the **Neon Cyan allowed region** of the track contracts in real-time.

The classical turning point brackets `[` and `]` close in on the particle on each oscillation. The accessible physical region of the universe shrinks until the allowed zone collapse is complete, clamping the particle to the bottom of the well.

Friction acts as a dynamic coordinator of classical boundaries.
