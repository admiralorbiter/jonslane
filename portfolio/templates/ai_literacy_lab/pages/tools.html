I use AI tools every week, but I try not to use them as a replacement for thinking. Assume I am being cautious and thoughtful: reading the PDF before asking for a summary, writing the first draft of an email before asking for help, and making the final call myself after getting feedback.

Of course, tools are not judged only by their careful users. Assume the worst about your laziest colleague, and the risks become easier to see. Like gun ownership, there will be responsible and irresponsible use. And in the wrong situation, careless use does not just risk your own foot. It can create bigger fallout for students, coworkers, readers, and institutions that trust the work.

System Configuration & Workflows

Below is a breakdown of how I use these tools in practice, contrasting active workflows with lazy shortcuts:

[✔] Chatbots: ChatGPT / Gemini / Claude
  • Active workflow: Requesting constructive feedback, voice alignment, or target critique on your drafts.
  • Lazy shortcut: Letting the chatbot write the first draft, producing sterile "AI slop."
  • Additional notes: Useful for quick questions (which can be better than Google search when done carefully) and summarizing documents.
[✔] Coding: Antigravity / Cursor / Claude Code / Codex
  • Active workflow: Reviewing, testing, and iterating inside a real development workflow (acting as the Editor rather than just a copy-paste Coder).
  • Lazy shortcut: Pretending the first answer is magic, and running unverified scripts that introduce technical debt.

[✔] Research: Deep Research / NotebookLM
  • Active workflow: Source-grounded synthesis, comparing info across multiple documents, and finding patterns, gaps, or better questions.
  • Lazy shortcut: Accepting summaries as absolute truth without checking the primary sources.
[✔] Learning & Other: NotebookLM
  • Active workflow: Using generated podcasts, summaries, videos, or everyday presentations as a second pass to re-engage with dry or complex texts.
  • Lazy shortcut: Relying on generated audio or slides as "first contact" to avoid reading the primary text.
