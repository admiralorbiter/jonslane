## 1. How to Use This List

This is not a list of sources that all carry equal authority.

- **Physics foundation:** use textbooks and canonical lectures to verify definitions, equations, and scope.
- **Physics-education research:** use research papers to identify learner difficulties and interaction-design implications.
- **Simulation precedents:** study what interactions already exist, then build a distinct page around your project's conceptual spine.
- **Indexes and quick references:** use these to find material, not as the sole authority for subtle claims.

## 2. Core Physics References

### The Feynman Lectures on Physics, Volume I

Main index:

- https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_toc.html

Most relevant chapters:

- Chapter 4, Conservation of Energy: https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_04.html
- Chapter 13, Work and Potential Energy: https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_13.html
- Chapter 14, Conservative Forces and Potentials: https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_14.html
- Chapter 21, Harmonic Oscillator: https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_21.html
- Chapter 24, Oscillator Energy and Damping: https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_24.html
- Chapter 52, Symmetry and Conservation Laws: https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/I_52.html

Use for:

- The abstract meaning of a conserved quantity.
- Energy as a sum of multiple calculable contributions.
- Work-energy relationships.
- Conservative forces and path independence.
- Gravitational and spring potential energy.
- Connections among oscillation, energy, and later physics.

Caution:

Feynman's presentation is conceptually rich but not always the best direct instructional sequence for a beginner-facing interactive. Use it to sharpen the underlying model, then rewrite in the project's own language.

### PhET Interactive Simulations

- https://phet.colorado.edu/
- Energy Skate Park: Basics: https://phet.colorado.edu/en/simulations/energy-skate-park-basics
- Research page: https://phet.colorado.edu/en/research

Use for:

- Studying direct manipulation of tracks and initial states.
- Energy bars and pie-chart representations.
- Visualizing kinetic, potential, thermal, and total energy.
- Comparing frictionless and frictional motion.
- Research-grounded simulation design.

Design lessons worth adapting:

- Make invisible quantities visible.
- Use immediate feedback.
- Keep the initial interface simple.
- Build implicit guidance into controls and constraints.
- Test with think-aloud interviews.
- Pair open exploration with a concrete prompt or goal.

Do not simply reproduce Energy Skate Park. Your distinct contribution should be the explicit system boundary, the potential-landscape interpretation, and the connection between physical conservation and numerical solver error.

### Falstad Math, Physics, and Engineering Applets

- https://www.falstad.com/mathphysics.html

Relevant applets include:

- Harmonic oscillator.
- Coupled oscillations.
- Ordinary differential equations.
- Orbital rendezvous.
- Lagrange points.
- Fourier series.
- One-dimensional quantum mechanics.
- Vector fields.

Use for:

- Studying compact, high-information scientific controls.
- Seeing how a single simulation can expose mathematical structure.

Caution:

Falstad applets often prioritize expert exploration and density of controls. Your beginner pathway should preserve the computational depth while using progressive disclosure and a more explicit learning sequence.

## 5. Physics-Education Research: Energy Understanding

### Suarez, Baccino, Marti, and Monteiro - Inconsistencies and Errors in Traditional Approaches to Energy

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.09480

Use for:

- A system-centered treatment of energy.
- Separating energy transfer from stored energy.
- Avoiding narrow presentations that treat mechanical-energy formulas as the entire conservation principle.

### Daane, McKagan, Vokos, and Scherr - Energy Conservation in Dissipative Processes

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.0379

Use for:

- The common difficulty of accepting imperceptible thermal energy.
- The instructional value of exaggerated comparison cases.
- Designing the friction chapter so the thermal destination is visible.

### McKagan and Wieman - Student Understanding of Energy in Quantum Tunneling

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0608244

Use for:

- Preventing the later misconception that particles lose energy while tunneling through a time-independent barrier.
- Clarifying what a potential-energy diagram represents.
- Designing the bridge from classical allowed regions to quantum behavior.

### Singh and Schunn - Conservation of Energy as a Pivotal Concept

- arXiv: https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06024

Use for:

- Evidence that energy is a high-leverage conceptual bridge.
- Identifying persistent learner difficulties that conventional instruction often leaves unresolved.

## 8. Source Review Checklist

Before importing a claim into a truth document:

- Is this a definition, measured fact, approximation, convention, or instructional choice?
- What system and regime does it assume?
- Is the source primary or merely summarizing another source?
- Does the equation have stated units and sign conventions?
- Are limits such as $v \ll c$, constant mass, or time-independent potential explicit?
- Does a newer or more complete framework modify the statement?
- Can the claim be validated numerically in the simulation?
- Does the planned visual accidentally imply more than the equation says?
