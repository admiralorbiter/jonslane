**Push Gravity from Particle Bombardment — When an Appealing Mechanism Creates Unobserved Consequences**

## Overview

Le Sage's theory of gravity (developed in the 18th century, with antecedents
in Fatio de Duillier's work) imagines space filled with tiny ultra-fast
particles moving in all directions. A single body is bombarded uniformly
from all sides — no net force. But two bodies partially shield each other:
each body intercepts some particles that would otherwise have hit the other,
creating an asymmetry that produces a net push toward the other body.

The theory is attractive because it gives a *mechanical* explanation for
gravity — action by contact, no mysterious action at a distance. And with
the right particle flux and cross-section assumptions, it does generate an
approximate inverse-square force.

---

## Central Question

A particle simulation can generate something resembling an inverse-square
force. But what other consequences does the mechanism predict — and are they
observed?

---

## The Theory's Self-Destruction

| Prediction | Le Sage | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Inverse-square force | Generated by shielding geometry | ✓ Matches |
| Gravitational drag | Particles hitting a moving body create net retarding force | No measurable orbital decay — rules out high-flux versions |
| Heating | Particle bombardment deposits kinetic energy | No observed gravitational heating |
| Gravitational aberration | Gravity should point toward where a body *was* (finite particle speed) | Gravity appears to act instantaneously — strong constraint |
| Shielding | Massive body between two others should reduce gravity | No gravitational shielding observed |
| Composition | Force on compound body depends on how constituents are arranged | Gravity depends only on total mass, not arrangement |

---

## What Makes This Interesting

Le Sage gravity is an example of a theory that:
- Generates its primary phenomenon (inverse-square attraction) from a
  plausible mechanism
- Generates several *additional* predictions that are not observed
- Cannot be easily modified to suppress the unwanted predictions without
  also suppressing the wanted one

This is a useful contrast with the ether case study, where modifications
*could* be made to survive each individual experiment. Le Sage gravity
faces a more structural failure: the mechanism that generates gravity
also necessarily generates drag and heating.

---

## Particle Simulation

A 2D simulation:
- Randomly moving particles filling the space
- Two massive bodies represented as discs that absorb/reflect particles
- Measure net force on each body: compare with inverse-square law
- Measure drag on a moving body
- Measure energy deposited in each body over time

## Implementation Notes

*A 2D particle simulation with configurable particle flux, body cross-section,
and body velocity. Force measurement by momentum transfer counting.
Computationally moderate — should be feasible with 10,000–50,000 particles
in a Web Worker.*
